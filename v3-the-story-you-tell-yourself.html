POSH

The Story You Tell Yourself

The first story is not always the true story.
Children need to learn that thoughts, guesses, fears, and assumptions can shape how they feel and react online.

Thinking Skill
CHECK THE STORY BEFORE YOU REACT
A child may not react to what happened. They may react to what they think it means.
POSH principle:
The story in your head can either calm you down or wind you up.

What this means

When something happens online, children quickly create meaning from it.

  • “They hate me.”
  • “Everyone is laughing at me.”
  • “I’m not good enough.”
  • “I have to reply now.”
  • “If I say no, they won’t like me.”
That story may feel true, but feelings are not always facts.

The story chain

Something happens
↓
Child creates a story
↓
Feeling grows
↓
Reaction follows
↓
Outcome changes

Unsafe stories children may tell themselves

  • “I need their approval.”
  • “I have to keep this secret.”
  • “I’ll be in trouble if I tell.”
  • “They are the only person who understands me.”
  • “If I block them, something bad will happen.”
  • “I deserve this.”

Why this matters for grooming and manipulation

Manipulators often try to control the child’s story.

They may make the child feel special, guilty, scared, responsible, ashamed, or dependent.

Once they control the story, they can influence the decision.

Control the story, control the reaction.

Better questions to teach

  • What else could this mean?
  • Am I guessing or do I know?
  • What would I tell a friend in this situation?
  • Is this thought helping me stay safe?
  • Do I need an adult to help me check this?

Scripts for kids

“This is the story my brain is telling me.”
“I don’t know for sure yet.”
“I can check before I react.”
“A scary thought is not the same as a fact.”
“I can ask a safe adult to help me sort this out.”

Parent scripts

“Let’s separate what happened from what your brain is saying it means.”
“That feeling makes sense, but let’s check the facts.”
“What is another possible explanation?”
“You don’t have to act on the first story.”

Final POSH reminder

The first thought is not always the truth.

The first feeling is not always the safest guide.

The first reaction is not always the best choice.

Teach children to check the story before they act.
