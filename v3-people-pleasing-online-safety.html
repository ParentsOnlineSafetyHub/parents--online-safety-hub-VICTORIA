People Pleasing & Online Safety

Children who struggle to disappoint others often struggle to protect themselves.
People pleasing is one of the most overlooked online safety risks.

POSH Safety Truth:
Children who cannot say no often become easier to manipulate.
Core Behaviour Skill
KINDNESS IS GOOD.
PEOPLE PLEASING IS NOT.
Many children learn that keeping others happy is more important than protecting themselves.
Safe children learn that disappointing someone is sometimes healthier than betraying themselves.

What is people pleasing?

  • Saying yes when you want to say no
  • Avoiding conflict at all costs
  • Feeling responsible for other people's feelings
  • Being afraid to disappoint others
  • Ignoring your own discomfort
  • Prioritising approval over safety
People pleasing often looks kind on the outside while creating risk on the inside.

How people pleasing creates risk

Need approval
↓
Fear conflict
↓
Difficulty saying no
↓
Ignore discomfort
↓
Manipulation risk

What it looks like online

  • Replying when they don't want to
  • Staying in chats they dislike
  • Sharing information to avoid upsetting someone
  • Accepting friend requests out of guilt
  • Keeping uncomfortable secrets
  • Feeling trapped in conversations

Why predators like people pleasers

They are easier to pressure.

They avoid confrontation.

They feel guilty saying no.

They often put other people first.

Manipulators look for compliance before they look for opportunity.

What parents should teach

  • You are allowed to say no.
  • You are allowed to leave conversations.
  • You are not responsible for everyone else's feelings.
  • Discomfort is information.
  • Your safety comes before someone else's approval.

Child safety scripts

"No thanks."
"I'm not comfortable with that."
"I'm leaving the chat."
"I don't want to do that."
"I said no."

Final POSH reminder

You can be kind.

You can be respectful.

You can still say no.

The safest children are not the most obedient. They are the ones who can protect their boundaries.
