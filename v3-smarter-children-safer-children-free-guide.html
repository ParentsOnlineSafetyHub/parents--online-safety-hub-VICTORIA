Why this guide exists

Online safety should be simple enough to use before fear takes over

Children are growing up inside games, apps, group chats, livestreams, private messages, algorithms and digital pressure. Many adults want to help, but they were never taught the patterns, the warning signs or the words children need to hear.

Children need words

Children cannot always explain pressure, shame, fear, secrecy or confusion. POSH helps adults teach simple words before a problem escalates.

Adults need patterns

The platform may change, but many risk patterns repeat: private access, secrecy, emotional pressure, gifts, shame, isolation and off-platform movement.

Families need calm

Calm conversations help children speak earlier. Panic and shame often make children hide more.

What the free guide teaches

The basics every safe adult should understand

This free guide gives the starting language for the POSH ecosystem. It does not try to teach everything at once. It gives every reader the first safe step.

Digital resilience

Helping children think, pause, ask questions, notice pressure and recover from digital mistakes without hiding.

Safe adult language

What adults can say so children feel safe enough to talk, even when they are embarrassed, scared or unsure.

Warning signs in context

Secrecy, sudden mood changes, deleted messages, hidden accounts, late-night contact and panic around devices are signals to slow down and look closer.

Skills that work online and offline

Confidence, boundaries, executive functioning, emotional regulation, frustration tolerance and thinking before clicking are lifelong safety skills.

Whole-circle safety

This guide is for everyone in a child’s safety circle

A child may speak to a teacher, grandparent, coach, older sibling, aunty, uncle, carer or family friend before they speak to a parent. That is why POSH teaches the whole circle.

Parents and step-parents

Build family rules, safer routines, device visibility and calm conversations that keep children talking.

Teachers, carers and coaches

Notice behaviour changes, disclosures, secrecy, emotional pressure and distress that may appear outside the home.

Grandparents and family networks

Use plain language to understand apps, games, devices, private messages and warning signs without needing to be a technology expert.

Starter language

Simple words children need to hear

Children often hide when they fear punishment. These words help keep the door open.

When something feels off

Try saying: “You are not in trouble for telling me. We can slow this down together.”

When they made a mistake

Try saying: “We will deal with the problem first. We can talk about rules after you are safe.”

When someone asked for secrecy

Try saying: “Safe adults do not need you to hide things from the people who protect you.”

When they feel embarrassed

Try saying: “Embarrassment is heavy, but you do not have to carry it alone.”

When to move faster

Most families should start with practical safety steps. Some situations need calm action now.

Move faster if there are threats, sexual requests, blackmail, pressure to keep secrets, requests for photos or talk about meeting in person.

Do not shame. Do not panic. Do not delete evidence first. Slow the contact down and get help.
